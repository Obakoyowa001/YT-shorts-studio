# Short #001 — "If You Opened a Bank in 1837"

**Series angle:** *"If you did ___ in the 1800s…"*. This is the same 5-Act formula as `CLAUDE.md`, set in a real historical scheme. It's history, shady money, and a real term taught at each step.
**Topic:** America's Free Banking Era (Michigan, 1837). Anyone could open a bank and print their own banknotes. That led to "wildcat banks", fake gold reserves, and bank runs.
**Delivery spec:** 9:16 vertical, **4K (2160×3840)**, 30 fps, ~64 s.

## 3. Voiceover Script

**Word count:** 234 · **Target rate:** ~220 WPM (3.65 words/sec) · **Est. duration:** ~64 s
Voice: warm, deadpan American male, conspiratorial "older brother" tone. Trim all pauses over 0.4 s. `[beat]` = 0.3 s pause, **bold** = emphasis.

> If you opened a bank in **Michigan** in **1837**, congratulations. You're **legally** allowed to print your own money. Your name, your face, a picture of your **dog**. Anything. There's just one rule. If someone brings your paper money back, you have to swap it for **real gold** or silver.
>
> So, step one, you build your bank where **nobody** can find it. Deep in the woods, past the bears, right next to the **wildcats**. Nobody can cash out if nobody can find the door. [beat] This is called a **wildcat bank**.
>
> Step two, the **inspector** is coming to count your gold. You don't have any gold. But the bank next door does. So **one box** of gold rides through the forest, one day ahead of the inspector. Every bank he visits looks **rich**.
>
> Step three, the box doesn't show up. So you fill a barrel with **nails** and **broken glass**, and put **one layer** of coins on top. The inspector opens it. [beat] Beautiful.
>
> Then **Gus** gets paid in your money. He walks **two days** into the city to buy boots. The shopkeeper opens a giant book listing every bank in America, finds yours, and **laughs**. Gus's dollar is now worth a **few cents**.
>
> Then the **whole town** shows up at once, wanting their gold. [beat] You have **nails**. This is called a **bank run**.
>
> And that's basically why you're not allowed to print your own money anymore.

**Terms taught:** banknote (implied), **wildcat bank**, **bank run**.
**Act map:** Act I hook 0–7.6 s · Act II the gold rule 7.6–13.3 s · Act III steps 1–3 13.3–43.8 s · Act IV Gus payoff 43.8–54.8 s · Act V run + sign-off 54.8–64 s.

## 6. Fact-Check Notes

- **Michigan General Banking Law (March 1837)** let almost anyone open a bank and issue banknotes. Michigan was one of the clearest examples of the US "Free Banking Era" (1837–1863).
- **"Wildcat banks"** were banks set up in remote places to make it hard to redeem their notes for gold or silver (specie). The "wildcats" origin of the name is popular folklore, which suits the joke.
- **Shared gold and nail barrels:** Michigan's bank commissioners reported specie being moved from bank to bank ahead of inspections, and boxes filled with nails and glass with a layer of coins on top.
- **Bank note reporters / counterfeit detectors** were real publications. Merchants used them to look up whether a bank's notes were good and at what discount.
- **The end:** the National Bank Acts (1863–64) and a 10% federal tax on state banknotes (1865) drove private and state banknotes out of circulation.
