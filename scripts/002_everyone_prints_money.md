# Short #002 — "What If Everyone Could Print Money?"

**Angle:** a hypothetical ("what if everyone could print their own money?") backed by real history, answering *why* you're not allowed to. Same 5-Act formula as `CLAUDE.md`, with the same cast as Short #001 (the Boss = "you", Gus, Mo, Inspector Pike).
**Format:** 9:16 vertical, **real 3D** (toy-style figures and sets in Blender), **sound effects only** (no music bed).

## 2. Voiceover Script

**Word count:** 214 · **Target rate:** ~220 WPM · **Est. duration:** ~58 s

> Imagine tomorrow, everyone is allowed to print their own money. Congratulations. You're a **millionaire**. So is your neighbour. So is **Gus**.
>
> Step one, you print a **million dollars** and run to the store. But Gus printed **ten million**, and he got there first. The shopkeeper isn't dumb. If everyone has a million, a loaf of bread isn't worth **one dollar** anymore. It's worth a **thousand**. This is called **inflation**.
>
> Step two, everyone prints more to keep up. The printers run all night. By Friday, one loaf costs a **wheelbarrow** of cash. This is called **hyperinflation**, and it really happened. In **Germany** in **1923**, kids built **towers** out of money. In **Zimbabwe**, prices **doubled every day**.
>
> Step three, the shopkeeper stops taking money at all. He wants **eggs**. Gus pays for a haircut with a **chicken**. This is called **barter**.
>
> So countries made one rule. Only **one bank** gets to print money, and it has to print **slowly**. This is called a **central bank**.
>
> Gus still has the chicken. [beat] And that's basically why you're not allowed to print your own money.

**Terms taught:** inflation, hyperinflation, barter, central bank.

## 5. Fact-Check Notes

- **Inflation from extra money:** printing more money without more goods raises prices. This is the standard quantity-of-money explanation.
- **Germany, 1923 (Weimar hyperinflation):** prices rose so fast that paper money lost nearly all its value. Photos from the time show children playing with and stacking bundles of banknotes.
- **Zimbabwe, 2008:** monthly inflation peaked in November 2008, with prices doubling roughly every 24 hours. The country later issued a 100-trillion-dollar note.
- **Barter during hyperinflation:** people fell back on trading goods and services when money stopped being accepted.
- **Central banks:** most countries give one central bank the job of issuing currency and controlling how fast the money supply grows.
